"
Who killed Aunt Agatha?

The situation

    Someone who lives in Dreadbury Mansion killed Aunt Agatha.
    Agatha, the butler, and Charles live in Dreadbury Mansion, and are the only people who live therein.
    A killer always hates his victim, and is never richer than his victim.
    Charles hates no one that Aunt Agatha hates.
    Agatha hates everyone except the butler.
    The butler hates everyone not richer than Aunt Agatha.
    The butler hates everyone Aunt Agatha hates.
    No one hates everyone.
    Agatha is not the butler.

The question is: who killed Aunt Agatha?

From: Pelletier, Seventy-five problems for testing automatic theorem provers (1986), problem 55
      TPTP problem PUZ001

---

examples/fol/dreadbury.txt proves that Agatha killed herself. Here the solver finds the killer instead: the clues
only describe the situation, and nobody is named as the killer.

Everyone is one of the people in the mansion. The unknowns lives(x), killed(x, y), hates(x, y) and richer(x, y)
are the relations between them, which the solver chooses. A person p is a possible killer if some choice of these
relations satisfies all clues, with killed(p, Agatha). The atom quantifier exists killed(_, _), .. ranges over every
choice of the relations, so killer(p) only depends on p.

The truth table has a single row, in which only killer(Agatha) is true: Agatha killed herself.

Run with:
    rsbdd examples/dreadbury.txt -t -ft
"

let clues :=
    (exists x # person(x) & lives(x) & killed(x, Agatha)) &
    lives(Agatha) & lives(Butler) & lives(Charles) &
    (forall x, y # person(x) & person(y) => (killed(x, y) => hates(x, y)) & (killed(x, y) => -richer(x, y))) &
    (forall x # person(x) => (hates(Agatha, x) => -hates(Charles, x))) &
    (forall x # person(x) => (-(x = Butler) => hates(Agatha, x))) &
    (forall x # person(x) => (-richer(x, Agatha) => hates(Butler, x))) &
    (forall x # person(x) => (hates(Agatha, x) => hates(Butler, x))) &
    (forall x # person(x) => exists y # person(y) & -hates(x, y)) &
    -(Agatha = Butler);

person(Agatha) -> true;
person(Butler) -> true;
person(Charles) -> true;

forall p # person(p) =>
    (killer(p) <=> exists lives(_), killed(_, _), hates(_, _), richer(_, _) # clues & killed(p, Agatha))
