"
The Lion and the Unicorn

The situation

    The Lion lies on Mondays, Tuesdays and Wednesdays, and tells the truth on the other days of the week.
    The Unicorn lies on Thursdays, Fridays and Saturdays, and tells the truth on the other days of the week.

    One day, Alice meets them both.
    The Lion says: 'Yesterday was one of my lying days.'
    The Unicorn says: 'Yesterday was one of my lying days.'

The question is: what day is it today?

From: Raymond Smullyan, What Is the Name of This Book? (1978)
      Ohlbach & Schmidt-Schauss, The Lion and the Unicorn (1985), TPTP problem PUZ005

---

today(d) is the unknown: the solver chooses exactly one day. A statement is true exactly when the speaker is not
lying today, so the Lion's claim lion_lies(y) about yesterday y holds exactly when -lion_lies(d) holds today.

The Lion can only say this on a Monday (lying about Sunday) or on a Thursday (truthfully, about Wednesday).
The Unicorn can only say this on a Thursday (lying about Wednesday) or on a Sunday (truthfully, about Saturday).
The truth table has a single row: today is Thursday.

Run with:
    rsbdd examples/lion_unicorn.txt -t -ft
"

"The days of the week, and the day before each day"
day(Monday) -> true;
day(Tuesday) -> true;
day(Wednesday) -> true;
day(Thursday) -> true;
day(Friday) -> true;
day(Saturday) -> true;
day(Sunday) -> true;

yesterday(Sunday, Monday) -> true;
yesterday(Monday, Tuesday) -> true;
yesterday(Tuesday, Wednesday) -> true;
yesterday(Wednesday, Thursday) -> true;
yesterday(Thursday, Friday) -> true;
yesterday(Friday, Saturday) -> true;
yesterday(Saturday, Sunday) -> true;

"The lying days"
lion_lies(Monday) -> true;
lion_lies(Tuesday) -> true;
lion_lies(Wednesday) -> true;

unicorn_lies(Thursday) -> true;
unicorn_lies(Friday) -> true;
unicorn_lies(Saturday) -> true;

"Today is one day of the week"
[today(Monday), today(Tuesday), today(Wednesday), today(Thursday), today(Friday), today(Saturday), today(Sunday)] = 1;

"Both say: 'yesterday was one of my lying days', which is true exactly when they are not lying today"
forall d, y # day(d) & day(y) & today(d) & yesterday(y, d) =>
    (lion_lies(y) <=> -lion_lies(d)) & (unicorn_lies(y) <=> -unicorn_lies(d))
