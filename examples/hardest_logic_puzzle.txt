"
The Hardest Logic Puzzle Ever

The situation

    Three gods A, B, and C are called, in no particular order, True, False, and Random.
    True always speaks truly, False always speaks falsely, but whether Random speaks truly or falsely is a completely
    random matter.
    Your task is to determine the identities of A, B, and C by asking three yes-no questions; each question must be
    put to exactly one god.
    The gods understand English, but will answer all questions in their own language, in which the words for yes and
    no are da and ja, in some order. You do not know which word means which.

From: George Boolos, The Hardest Logic Puzzle Ever (1996)
      https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hardest_Logic_Puzzle_Ever

---

A situation w(a, b, c, m) states that A, B and C are a, b and c, and that da means m.
A yes-no question is true or false in each situation: the unknown true_in(q, s) is the truth of the question q in the
situation s. The solver chooses every question, and the god it is put to: asks(q, g).

The questions are the nodes of a tree: the first question is Q1, and the question after the answer da (or ja) to the
question q is da(q) (or ja(q)). After three answers you state the identities: states(q, g, r) states that god g is r.
The puzzle is solved if, in every situation and whatever Random does, the stated identities are correct.
Whether Random speaks truly to the question q is the unknown random(q), which is universally quantified.

There are many strategies, so ask for a single model. Variables marked * can be either value.

Run with:
    rsbdd examples/hardest_logic_puzzle.txt -m -v

The questions of the model, as a readable decision tree:
    rsbdd examples/hardest_logic_puzzle.txt -m -v | python3 examples/hardest_logic_puzzle.py
"

god(A) -> true;
god(B) -> true;
god(C) -> true;

role(True) -> true;
role(False) -> true;
role(Random) -> true;

meaning(Yes) -> true;
meaning(No) -> true;

"A, B and C are called, in no particular order, True, False and Random, and da means yes or no"
sum a, b, c, m # situation(w(a, b, c, m)) ->
    role(a) & role(b) & role(c) & -(a = b) & -(a = c) & -(b = c) & meaning(m);

sum a, b, c, m, r # is(A, w(a, b, c, m), r) -> r = a;
sum a, b, c, m, r # is(B, w(a, b, c, m), r) -> r = b;
sum a, b, c, m, r # is(C, w(a, b, c, m), r) -> r = c;
sum a, b, c, m, x # da_means(w(a, b, c, m), x) -> x = m;

"True always speaks truly, False always speaks falsely, whether Random speaks truly is random"
sum q # speaks_truly(True, q) -> true;
sum q # speaks_truly(False, q) -> false;
sum q # speaks_truly(Random, q) -> random(q);

"The answer of the god that is asked the question q in the situation s, in their own language"
sum q, s # answers_yes(q, s) ->
    exists g, r # god(g) & role(r) & asks(q, g) & is(g, s, r) & (speaks_truly(r, q) <=> true_in(q, s));
sum q, s # says_da(q, s) -> if answers_yes(q, s) then da_means(s, Yes) else da_means(s, No);

"After n more questions from the question q, the identities are determined in the situation s"
sum q, s # determined(q, s, Zero) ->
    forall g, r # god(g) & role(r) => (states(q, g, r) <=> is(g, s, r));
sum q, s, n # determined(q, s, More(n)) ->
    [asks(q, A), asks(q, B), asks(q, C)] = 1 &
    forall random(q) # if says_da(q, s) then determined(da(q), s, n) else determined(ja(q), s, n);

"Three questions determine the identities in every situation"
forall a, b, c, m # situation(w(a, b, c, m)) => determined(Q1, w(a, b, c, m), More(More(More(Zero))))
