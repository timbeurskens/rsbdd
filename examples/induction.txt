"
Proofs by induction over peano numerals.

Term rules decide an equation by rewriting both sides to normal form. That is not enough for x + 0 = x: plus recurses
on its first argument, so plus(x, _0) cannot be rewritten while x is unknown. 'induct x # l = r' proves the
equation for every natural number x by structural induction instead. The cases are the patterns of the rules of plus
at the position of x:

    case _0:      plus(_0, _0)      = _0         by rewriting
    case suc(x'): plus(suc(x'), _0) = suc(x')    by rewriting with the hypothesis plus(x', _0) -> x'

Every proven equation becomes a rewrite rule (a lemma) for the theorems that follow it. Commutativity cannot be
proven by induction from the definition of plus alone: its proof uses the first two theorems as lemmas.
Every theorem is proven, resulting in a single 'true' node in the BDD.

Run with:
    rsbdd examples/induction.txt
"

sum y # plus(_0, y) -> y;
sum x, y # plus(suc(x), y) -> suc(plus(x, y));
sum y # times(_0, y) -> _0;
sum x, y # times(suc(x), y) -> plus(y, times(x, y));

"0 is a right identity of addition"
induct x # plus(x, _0) = x;

"the successor can be moved out of the second argument"
induct x, y # plus(x, suc(y)) = suc(plus(x, y));

"addition is commutative"
induct x, y # plus(x, y) = plus(y, x);

"addition is associative"
induct x, y, z # plus(plus(x, y), z) = plus(x, plus(y, z));

"0 is a right zero of multiplication"
induct x # times(x, _0) = _0
