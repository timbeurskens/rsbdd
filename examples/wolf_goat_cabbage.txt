"
Wolf, goat and cabbage

The situation

    A farmer wants to cross a river with a wolf, a goat and a cabbage.
    The boat only has room for the farmer and one of the three.
    Left alone, the wolf eats the goat, and the goat eats the cabbage.

The question is: can the farmer bring everything to the other bank?

From: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf,_goat_and_cabbage_problem

---

Every actor has one bit for its bank: false is the left bank, true is the right bank.
The current state is (pf, pw, pg, pc), the next state is (f, w, g, c), for the farmer, wolf, goat and cabbage.

A crossing moves the farmer, and at most one item that was on the farmer's bank.
A state is safe if the goat is not left alone with the wolf or with the cabbage.

The least fixpoint computes all states that can be reached from the start (everything on the left bank)
using only safe crossings, without knowing the number of crossings in advance.
The result has 10 reachable states, including f & w & g & c: everything on the right bank, so the puzzle is solvable.

The fixpoint shows that the goal can be reached, but not which moves get there. One of the two 7-move solutions:
    goat over, return, wolf over, goat back, cabbage over, return, goat over.

Run with:
    rsbdd examples/wolf_goat_cabbage.txt -t -ft
"

let init := -f & -w & -g & -c;

"The goat is never alone with the wolf or the cabbage"
let safe := -((w <=> g) & (f ^ g)) & -((g <=> c) & (f ^ g));

"T(p, x): the farmer crosses, taking at most one item from his own bank"
let T :=
    (f ^ pf) &
    ((w ^ pw) => (pw <=> pf)) &
    ((g ^ pg) => (pg <=> pf)) &
    ((c ^ pc) => (pc <=> pf)) &
    [w ^ pw, g ^ pg, c ^ pc] <= 1 &
    safe;

"x is reachable if it is the initial state, or some reachable state p has a crossing to x"
lfp R # init | exists pf, pw, pg, pc # R[f := pf, w := pw, g := pg, c := pc] & T
